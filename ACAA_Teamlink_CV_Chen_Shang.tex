\sectionrule{RESEARCH PROFILE}
Chen Shang is a Ph.D. student at the University of Technology Sydney (UTS), Australia, under the supervision of \href{https://sites.google.com/view/dinh-thai-hoang/home}{Prof. Hoang Dinh}. His research focuses on deep learning-enabled wireless communication and sensing systems, mainly Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC), multi-modal sensing, and intelligent wireless networks. He has authored or co-authored \textbf{14 top-tier IEEE papers}, including \textbf{5 first-author JCR Q1 journal/magazine papers} and \textbf{2 first-author IEEE flagship conference/workshop papers}. His current research is supported by the Australian National Road Safety Program through the project \href{https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/projects/assistive-technologies-for-young-people-safety-on-two-wheelers/}{``Assistive Technologies for Young People Safety on Two-Wheelers,''} where his work on wireless communication and sensing systems aims to enable low-power, real-time, and reliable perception of road hazards and potential risks for safer young two-wheeler mobility.

\sectionrule{SELECTED RESEARCH IMPACT}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AI-enabled road safety and assistive sensing:} Recent IEEE JSAC~\hyperlink{pub:jsac}{[1]} work is supported by the project \href{https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/projects/assistive-technologies-for-young-people-safety-on-two-wheelers/}{``Assistive Technologies for Young People Safety on Two-Wheelers,''} funded by the National Road Safety Program under the Australian Government's Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communications, and the Arts (Grant No. NRSAGP-TI1-A48). As one of the top-ranked JCR Q1 journals in Telecommunications, IEEE JSAC highlights the research excellence and real-world relevance of this work. This project aims to develop AI-enabled multi-modal sensing technologies for detecting road hazards and potential risks in real time, improving safety for young two-wheeler riders.
  \item \textbf{Low-power and mobile sensing systems:} Research on ISAC, sensing-assisted wireless information and power transfer, and aerial-to-ground mobile platforms supports practical sensing systems that can operate under energy, mobility, and communication constraints. These techniques are relevant to road safety, smart transportation, environmental monitoring, and distributed sensor networks.
  \item \textbf{Human-centric digital twin and BCI research:} Work on brain-computer interface signal processing, neuromorphic computing, and personalized federated learning supports future immersive communications, assistive human-machine interaction, and human digital twins, where sensing signals can be translated into personalized and responsive digital services.
  \item \textbf{Privacy-aware intelligent systems:} Exploratory work on privacy-preserving machine learning and homomorphic encryption supports trustworthy collaboration over sensitive sensing, mobility, and human-related data, which is important for deploying AI systems beyond laboratory environments.
  \item \textbf{Cross-institution collaboration:} Research experience and collaborations with UTS, University of Canberra, HKUST(GZ), and SUTD have supported interdisciplinary work across wireless communications, intelligent sensing, edge intelligence, and safety-oriented applications.
\end{itemize}
